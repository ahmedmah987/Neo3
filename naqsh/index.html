How does it work?

Start with a tiling of regular polygons. From the midpoint of every edge, two lines leave in an X, each meeting the edge at the contact angle θ. Each line runs into its polygon until it meets the line from the neighbouring edge, and a star appears inside every polygon.

One rule, one angle. Changing θ alone takes the pattern from a quiet lattice to sharp stars. With interlacing on, each band passes over the band it crosses and under the next one, in turn, as in zellige tilework and carved wood.

The numbers under each tiling list the polygons that meet at every vertex: 4.6.12 is a square, a hexagon and a dodecagon.

E. H. Hankin described this method in 1925 as an account of how craftsmen drew Islamic patterns. Craig Kaplan turned it into an algorithm in 2005.
